# Near Gems privacy policy

_Last updated: 22 October 2026._

Near Gems finds interesting places near where you are. This policy says what it does with your
information, and what it does not do. The developer is Swaroop (gnanaswaroop@gmail.com).

## Summary

- Near Gems has no accounts, no sign-in, no ads, and no analytics or crash-reporting services.
- Your location is used to find places near you. It is sent to the place-data services described
  below, and the developer never receives it.
- Places you have visited, places you have seen, and your settings are stored only on your phone.
- Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared with advertisers.

## Location

Near Gems asks for your location so it can find nearby places and show how far away they are and
which way they are. Before Android asks for permission, the app explains this on its own screen.

While the app is open, your position is used on your phone to work out distances and direction.
It is also sent to:

- the OpenStreetMap Overpass API, to find restaurants, cafés, bars and other places near you;
- Wikipedia (the MediaWiki API), to find landmarks near you;
- tile.openstreetmap.org, when the mini map is shown.

These requests come from your phone, so the services can see your IP address and the position in
each request, as they would for any web request. The developer does not receive or store your
location.

Near Gems uses the Google Play services location library to read your position from the phone's
sensors. That library is provided by Google and is subject to Google's privacy policy.

**Car watch (optional, off by default).** If you turn on car watch in Settings, Near Gems keeps
checking your location in the background, with a notification shown while it does, so it can
announce places when your car connects. Turn it off in Settings to stop this. Near Gems does not
use background location for advertising or tracking.

If you turn off location, Near Gems cannot show suggestions.

## Photos

Landmark photos are loaded from Wikimedia servers when a place is shown. Each photo's licence is on
its file page, which the app links to. The image loader keeps recently viewed photos in a cache in
the app's own storage on your phone, and clearing the app's data removes that cache.

## Data stored on your phone

Near Gems keeps the following on your phone only. None of it is uploaded.

- **Visit history.** When you come within about 50 metres of a place, Near Gems remembers it.
- **Seen places.** Each place is marked New or Seen. Seen places are kept in a small local database
  (SQLite), so the badges stay the same after you close the app.
- **Dismissed places.** Places you pass with Next are hidden for 12 hours.
- **Settings.** Categories, search distance, the one-card or list choice, theme, car options, and
  any test location you set.

Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app removes all of this.

## Third-party services

- OpenStreetMap data and map tiles are © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open
  Database Licence (ODbL). Their privacy practices are described at openstreetmap.org.
- Wikipedia article text is available under CC BY-SA. Photos come from Wikimedia at runtime.
- Google Play services provides the location functions described above.

## Security

Requests to the services above use HTTPS. Data on your phone stays in the app's own storage, which
other apps cannot read.

## Children

Near Gems is not directed at children, and it does not knowingly collect information from anyone,
including children under 13.

## Your choices

- Turn off location in Android's settings at any time. Suggestions stop, and nothing is sent.
- Turn off car watch in Near Gems' Settings.
- Clear the app's data or uninstall the app to remove everything stored on your phone.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at the same address with a new date.
